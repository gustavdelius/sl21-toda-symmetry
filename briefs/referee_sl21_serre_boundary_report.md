## 1. Normalization and a fresh Wick calculation

Use $X(z)X(w)\sim-\log(z-w)$ and

\[
a_0=(ib/\sqrt2,b/\sqrt2,i),\qquad
a_2=(ib/\sqrt2,-b/\sqrt2,-i),\qquad
t=b^2,\quad d=-(a_0+a_2)/t,\quad \rho=2/t.
\]

Direct scalar products give $a_0^2=a_2^2=-1$, $a_0a_2=1-t$, $a_0d=a_2d=1$, and $d^2=-\rho$. Thus

\[
J_A=:e^{-dX}:,\qquad J_B=:C e^{dX}:,\qquad C=a_0\partial X,
\]

have $CC\sim(z-w)^{-2}$, while contracting $C$ with a Yukawa exponential gives $\beta/(z-w)$ for node 2, where

\[
\beta=t-1,\qquad \eta=t-2=\beta-1.
\]

Here $\eta$ is a coupling polynomial, not a cutoff. The action and tail normalization is

\[
S_{\rm int}=\pi^{-1}\int d^2w\,(\lambda_0Y_0+\lambda_2Y_2+O(m^2)),
\qquad H_B^{(1)}=-t\lambda_2K_2,
\quad K_2=:e^{(d+a_2)X+a_2\bar X}:.
\]

The current-first cocycle for momentum $nd$ and a full Yukawa is $(-1)^n$. The signs below include it. Both currents are even. The common charge factors $(2\pi i)^{-n}$ are suppressed in the ordered density-product and counterterm formulas, on both sides.

For $B(z_1)B(z_2)B(z_3)A(a)$ define

\[
\mathcal R=\prod_{i<j}(z_i-z_j)^\rho\prod_i(z_i-a)^{-\rho},
\qquad G(w)=\frac{(a-w)^2}{\prod_i(w-z_i)^2}.
\tag{B1}
\]

To reconstruct the scalar Wick coefficient, set

\[
f_i=-\sum_{j\ne i}\frac1{z_i-z_j}+\frac1{z_i-a}+\frac\beta{z_i-w}.
\]

The polynomial is $f_1f_2f_3+\sum_{i<j}f_k/(z_i-z_j)^2$. Multiplication by the source vertex factor $(a-w)/\prod_i(z_i-w)$ reduces it to

\[
\mathcal K=\frac{\beta\eta}{\rho}\partial_a[\mathcal R G(w)],
\tag{B2}
\]

because $\partial_a\log\mathcal R=\rho\sum_i(z_i-a)^{-1}$ and $2/\rho=t$. The action weight is $-\lambda_2/\pi$. For a tail at $z_i$, the two remaining derivatives have external contractions $1/(z_j-a)+\eta/(z_j-z_i)$. Including the tail coefficient and cocycle gives

\[
\frac{t\lambda_2\eta}{\rho}\partial_a[\mathcal R S_i],
\qquad S_i=(a-z_i)^2D_i,\quad
D_i=\prod_{j\ne i}(z_i-z_j)^{-2}.
\tag{B3}
\]

The node-0 scalar coefficient vanishes: its Wick polynomial has $\beta=1$ and contains $\beta(\beta-1)$. These are identities of the leading coefficient, multiplying $\mathcal V_2=:e^{(2d+a_2)X+a_2\bar X}:$. Differentiating that normal field, a spectator, or a local partition function is a higher Taylor level; it must not be included in (B2) without its additional terms.

`kernel_audit.py` reconstructs the Wick polynomials independently and tests (B2)–(B3) with symbolic $a,w,t$ at two asymmetric three-$B$ configurations. The derivation above does not specialize those positions.

## 2. Integrate the source with its finite holes retained

Write $z_i=x_i+i\tau_i$ and put

\[
\ell_i=\sum_{j\ne i}(z_i-z_j)^{-1},\qquad
m_i=\sum_{j\ne i}(z_i-z_j)^{-2}.
\]

The simple residue and regular Laurent coefficient of $G$ at $z_i$ are

\[
R_i=-2D_i[(a-z_i)+(a-z_i)^2\ell_i],
\]
\[
T_i=D_i[1+4(a-z_i)\ell_i+(a-z_i)^2(2\ell_i^2+m_i)].
\tag{B4}
\]

Equivalently, if $Q_i=(a-w)^2/\prod_{j\ne i}(w-z_j)^2$, these are $Q_i'(z_i)$ and $Q_i''(z_i)/2$. Residues at infinity give $\sum_iR_i=0$ and $\sum_i z_iR_i=-S$, where $S=\sum_iS_i$.

Use four time levels $c\epsilon(3/2,1/2,-1/2,-3/2)$ in operator-word order, with $c>2$. Treat the outermost current pole by its circular Cauchy finite part. Remove radius-$\epsilon$ source disks at the three inner currents. All finite disks are disjoint, even when their real coordinates coincide. Let $\mathcal I_B$ be the set of $B$ currents in inner slots. Green's theorem gives exactly

\[
\frac1\pi\int_{\rm holes}G(w)\,d^2w
=-\sum_i\bar z_iR_i-\epsilon^2\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_B}T_i
=S+2i\sum_i\tau_iR_i-\epsilon^2\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_B}T_i.
\tag{B5}
\]

The infinity boundary is zero here. A disk around $a$ contributes zero, since $G$ is holomorphic there and $G(a)=0$. Its moving boundary also gives zero for the scalar derivative, since $\partial_a[\mathcal R G](w=a)=0$. Thus this exclusion is accounted for, rather than dropped on dimensional grounds.

Combining (B2), (B3), and (B5), the regulated scalar density is $\partial_a\mathcal P$, with

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\mathcal P=\frac{\lambda_2\eta}{\rho}\mathcal R
\left[S-2i\beta\sum_i\tau_iR_i
+\beta\epsilon^2\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_B}T_i\right].}
\tag{B6}
\]

In a joint collision, $S$, $\tau R_i$, and $\epsilon^2T_i$ all have the same degree. This rules out using the source-first real-axis expression alone in that limit. Direct trapezoidal integration of the actual circular Green boundaries at complex, asymmetric positions agrees with (B5) to $3.3\times10^{-14}$ in the independent check.

## 3. Evaluate the scalar endpoint sum

First take all three $B$ integrations on $I=[L,R]$ and the $A$ integration on $J$, with $I$ strictly inside $J$. The integrated derivative has only the two $A$ endpoints. At either endpoint the proper $BB$ and $BBB$ relative degrees are $\rho-1$ and $3\rho-2$. Hence the endpoint integrals converge for $\operatorname{Re}\rho>1$. The height and hole corrections vanish in this domain. This conclusion includes the regions where two or three $B$ positions are separated only by $O(\epsilon)$, not just fixed distinct positions.

Let $W_k=B^{3-k}AB^k$, $k=0,1,2,3$, and define

\[
s=e^{-i\pi\rho},\quad c_s=1+s+s^{-1},\quad
(d_0,d_1,d_2,d_3)=(1,-c_s,c_s,-1).
\]

Relative to $W_0$, the left-endpoint factor is $s^k$ and the right-endpoint factor is $s^{-k}$. These factors follow by evaluating the fractional powers in actual word order. Integer powers in the tail formulas are accompanied by the cocycle signs above. Now

\[
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^3d_kx^k
=(1-x)(1-sx)(1-s^{-1}x),\qquad p(s)=p(s^{-1})=0.
\tag{B7}
\]

The two endpoint contributions therefore cancel separately. No finite quartic contact was added to enforce this cancellation.

An independent numerical check integrates (B6) over all three $B$ coordinates on $[-1,1]$, with $A$ endpoints $\pm2$, $\rho=\sqrt3$, and $c=\sqrt5$. It retains the word-dependent heights and inner-hole masks. For one Sobol scramble, the absolute word sum at $\epsilon=.04,.02,.01,.005,.0025$ is respectively

~~~text
0.25612774  0.18156004  0.09818975  0.04980034  0.02485544
~~~

A second independent scramble gives $0.02652710$ at the last cutoff; their complex-value difference is $0.00179427$. These are finite-cutoff trend checks, not a precise numerical extrapolation to zero or a computation of a complete correlation function. The zero follows from the endpoint argument.

### Equal endpoints need an additional prescription

One cannot transfer the preceding absolute-convergence argument to identical spatial intervals: an $A/B$ endpoint collision introduces an incompatible restriction. A useful explicit scalar prescription is to replace only the $BB$ exponent by an independent complex parameter $g$:

\[
\mathcal R_{g,\rho}=\prod_{i<j}(z_i-z_j)^g\prod_i(z_i-a)^{-\rho},
\qquad t=2/\rho.
\tag{B8}
\]

This is an auxiliary analytic regulator of the scalar kernels, not a new physical Gram matrix. The derivative identities (B2)–(B6) remain valid because their $a$ derivative does not act on the $BB$ factors. For $\operatorname{Re}g>1$, $\operatorname{Re}\rho<0$, the same regulated scalar integrals with identical finite intervals have a common convergence domain. Relevant interior relative degrees are

\[
g-1\ (BB),\quad 3g-2\ (BBB),\quad
-\rho\ (AB),\quad 1+g-2\rho\ (ABB),\quad
3g-3\rho\ (BBBA).
\tag{B9}
\]

They are positive in that domain. Fixing the $A$ endpoint gives positive endpoint degrees $1-\rho$, $2+g-2\rho$, and $1+3g-3\rho$. The height and hole terms obey the same joint bounds, so they vanish after integration. There is no unexamined equal-endpoint limit in this initial domain.

The endpoint phase factors in (B7) depend on $\rho$, not on $g$. Consequently the scalar sum is zero throughout this two-variable domain. Continue the individual left-hand scalar integrals meromorphically, with their ordinary Taylor subtractions, and then restrict to $g=\rho$ at regular generic points. This defines the scalar identity for equal intervals. The full-cluster primitive has positive relative degree one on the diagonal; defining its coefficient as its distributional $a$ derivative fixes the possible marginal scalar contact. No independent finite term is supplied at that face.

This construction also fixes the $B$-only scalar/jet extensions from their $\operatorname{Re}g>1$ domain. It does **not** prove a full ultraviolet or massive quartic identity off the diagonal $g=\rho$. The complete permutation identity need not hold when the pair exponents are independent. Nor does it establish that a bare equal-interval cutoff at physical $g=\rho$ already implements this finite part. Those distinctions are essential to the scope of the result.

`kernel_audit.py` checks the actual endpoint branches with independent $g$, and numerically integrates the scalar $a$ derivative across all three branch points on a common interval in this initial domain. These checks concern scalar kernels only.

## 4. Lower products: all singular Taylor levels in a stated strip

Work at generic $-1/3<\operatorname{Re}\rho<0$. Exclude poles of the continued distributions and all logarithmic resonance prescriptions. More negative $\rho$ requires additional Taylor levels. Write

\[
\mathcal V_n=:e^{(nd+a_2)X+a_2\bar X}:.
\]

The source-area projection used to derive the coefficients can be checked without importing a previous product formula. For distinct real $z_i$, $F(w)=\prod_i(w-z_i)^{-2}$, and a polynomial $P(w,\bar w)$ of total degree less than $2n-2$, Green's theorem and the vanishing residue at infinity give

\[
\pi^{-1}\operatorname{FP}\int F(w)P(w,\bar w)\,d^2w
=\sum_iD_iP(z_i,z_i).
\tag{B10}
\]

For a monomial, integrate $\bar w^q$ once in $\bar w$, then sum the resulting simple residues. The boundary at infinity vanishes under the displayed degree restriction. This proves (B10) and supplies independent moment checks for all levels used here.

At each pole the action double-pole coefficient is $\beta$ times the corresponding fused-tail contraction. The tail coefficient is $t$. Thus the combined low jets are the sum of tail kernels divided by $t$, because $t-\beta=1$. The relevant numerator degrees satisfy (B10). Far-source contributions start at higher collision degree and do not change these divergent local jets.

### The pair

In midpoint coordinates $z_{1,2}=y\pm r/2$, the scalar coefficient is $2\lambda_2\eta r^{\rho-2}\mathcal V_2(y)$. Its first normal-field Taylor jet vanishes identically. In particular this is a cancellation, not a reason to omit endpoint expansion. On the ordered interval product, $y\in[L+r/2,R-r/2]$. With a gap cutoff $r>\epsilon$, the complete divergent local subtraction in the present strip is

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
CT_{BB}=\lambda_2\left[
\frac{2\eta}{\rho-1}\epsilon^{\rho-1}\int_L^R\mathcal V_2(y)\,dy
-\frac\eta\rho\epsilon^\rho\bigl(\mathcal V_2(L)+\mathcal V_2(R)\bigr)
\right].}
\tag{B11}
\]

It is **added** to the cutoff integral. The first coefficient is $-2t$ and agrees with the earlier leading subtraction. The endpoint term is required even though the bulk first jet is zero. The next radial degree is $\rho+1>0$.

A nonconstant test with $\mathcal V_2(y)=e^{0.3y}$ and $\rho=-\sqrt2/10$ converges after both terms are included; its errors at $\epsilon=.08,.04,.02,.01$ are $0.05571,0.030917,0.017104,0.0094472$, with the predicted $\epsilon^{\rho+1}$ ratio. This tests a subtraction coefficient, not a physical expectation value of $\mathcal V_2$.

### The triple before angular integration

Use centroid positions $z_i=y+r_i$, $\sum_i r_i=0$, and define

\[
C_2=a_0\partial^2X,\quad D=d\partial X,\quad D_2=d\partial^2X,
\quad U=a_2\bar\partial\bar X,\quad U_2=a_2\bar\partial^2\bar X,
\]
\[
F_1=-tD-C+U,\quad F_2=-tD_2-C_2+U_2,\quad
M_j=\sum_i r_i^jD_i,\quad P_2=\sum_i r_i^2.
\]

After stripping $\lambda_2\prod_{i<j}(r_i-r_j)^\rho\mathcal V_3$, the three potentially divergent levels are

\[
K_0=-\eta(\eta-1)M_0,
\qquad K_1=-\eta[(\eta-1)F_1-3C]M_1,
\]
\[
K_2=-\eta\left[
\left(\frac{\eta-1}{2}D_2+C_2\right)P_2M_0
+\left(\frac{\eta-1}{2}(F_2+F_1^2)-3CF_1\right)M_2
\right].
\tag{B12}
\]

All field products are normal ordered with $\mathcal V_3$. To verify the general-position formula, the tail at $r_i$ leaves two derivatives at $r_j,r_k$ with polynomial

\[
C_jC_k+\frac{C_j-C_k}{r_j-r_k}
+\eta\left(\frac{C_j}{r_k-r_i}+\frac{C_k}{r_j-r_i}\right)
+\frac{\eta(\eta-1)}{(r_j-r_i)(r_k-r_i)}.
\]

Multiply by $-[(r_j-r_i)(r_k-r_i)]^{-1}$ and expand its exponential. The identities $\sum_i[(r_j-r_i)(r_k-r_i)]^{-1}=0$, $r_j+r_k=-r_i$, and $r_j(r_j-r_i)+r_k(r_k-r_i)=P_2$ give (B12). The same calculation at node 0 gives zero through these levels; for the pair it gives zero through level one. This explicitly checks which descendants are absent.

### The triple with a span cutoff and hard endpoints

Set $r=z_1-z_3>0$, $x=(z_1-z_2)/r$, $0<x<1$. Unit-span centroid offsets are $(1+x,1-2x,-2+x)/3$. The radial measure is $r^{3\rho-3}dr$, and the angular weight is $[x(1-x)]^\rho$. Write $\ell=(2-x)/3$, $h=(1+x)/3$; the centroid domain is $[L+r\ell,R-rh]$.

Let $B_0=B(\rho-1,\rho-1)$ and define the following meromorphically continued angular moments, initially integrated for $\operatorname{Re}\rho>1$:

\[
I_0=B_0\frac{3\rho-1}{2\rho-1},\qquad
J_0=B_0\frac{9\rho^2+2\rho-1}{6(2\rho-1)(2\rho+1)},
\]
\[
I_{1h}=-B_0\frac{3\rho+1}{12(2\rho-1)(2\rho+1)},\qquad
I_P=B_0\frac{\rho(3\rho+1)}{(2\rho-1)(2\rho+1)},\qquad I_2=I_P/6.
\tag{B13}
\]

They are respectively the integrals of $M_0$, $M_0h^2$, $M_1h$, $P_2M_0$, and $M_2$, evaluated at unit span. Also $\int M_1=0$, $\int M_1\ell=-I_{1h}$, $\int M_0\ell=I_0/2$, and $\int M_0\ell^2=J_0$, with the weight understood. Expanding the rational numerators in beta moments proves these expressions. Separate direct finite-part quadrature verifies their continuation to $\rho=-\sqrt2/10$, rather than only their positive-coupling values.

Define the Wick fields

\[
O_1=[(\eta-1)F_1-3C]\mathcal V_3,\quad
O_{2a}=[(\eta-1)D_2/2+C_2]\mathcal V_3,
\]
\[
O_{2b}=[(\eta-1)(F_2+F_1^2)/2-3CF_1]\mathcal V_3,
\quad \Delta f=f(R)-f(L).
\]

The radial Taylor coefficients, including the changing centroid domain, are

\[
A_0=-\eta(\eta-1)I_0\int_L^R\mathcal V_3,
\qquad A_1=\frac{\eta(\eta-1)I_0}{2}[\mathcal V_3(L)+\mathcal V_3(R)],
\]
\[
A_2=-\eta\left[I_P\int_L^R O_{2a}+I_2\int_L^R O_{2b}\right]
-\frac{\eta(\eta-1)J_0}{2}\Delta(\partial_y\mathcal V_3)
+\eta I_{1h}\Delta O_1.
\tag{B14}
\]

For the span cutoff $r>\epsilon$, add

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle CT_{BBB}=\lambda_2\sum_{j=0}^2
\frac{\epsilon^{3\rho-2+j}}{3\rho-2+j}A_j.}
\tag{B15}
\]

The remaining radial degree is $3\rho+1>0$. The angular finite parts include the proper $BB$ subfaces and are fixed by continuation from their convergent domain. This is a specified angular-then-radial analytic prescription. Formula (B15) is not asserted to be the identical counterterm representative for an arbitrary simultaneous sharp pair-gap cutoff. Such a conversion may change finite terms and must be calculated.

## 5. A proper $ABB$ face has a different infinity term

For two $B$ positions, put $\mathcal R_2=(z_1-z_2)^\rho\prod_{i=1}^2(z_i-a)^{-\rho}$ and $S_2=\sum_{i=1}^2(a-z_i)^2/(z_1-z_2)^2$. Now $G_2=(a-w)^2/\prod_{i=1}^2(w-z_i)^2$ has a nonzero $w^{-2}$ coefficient. Residues of $wG_2$ give

\[
\sum_i z_i\operatorname{Res}_{z_i}G_2+S_2=1,\qquad
\pi^{-1}\operatorname{FP}\int G_2\,d^2w=S_2-1
\tag{B16}
\]

for real resolved positions. The action sign is positive here, because the net current charge is $d$ and its source cocycle is $-1$. Its coefficient is $\lambda_2\beta\partial_a[\mathcal R_2(S_2-1)]/\rho$. The two tails sum to $-t\lambda_2\partial_a[\mathcal R_2S_2]/\rho$. Consequently the combined scalar coefficient is

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle -\frac{\lambda_2}{\rho}\partial_a[\mathcal R_2(S_2+\beta)].}
\tag{B17}
\]

The $\beta$ term must not be omitted. This is a resolved scalar primitive, not yet the full operator extension of this face. For node 0 the resolved integrated scalar vanishes: $2\int(a-w)F+\sum_i(z_i-a)^{-1}\int(a-w)^2F=0$ by the same moments. Finite-height and finite-hole power contacts must still be treated according to the selected analytic prescription.

## 6. What this does and does not close

The leading marginal $BBBA/Y_2$ boundary is now evaluated. It is compatible with the quartic coefficients obtained earlier and needs no separately tuned scalar four-current contact in the prescription above. The lower-product subtraction data are also substantially more complete.

The remaining first-order task is to assemble these **operator-valued** lower subtractions inside all four words using one contour prescription. In particular one must check their overlapping $BB$, $BBB$, $AB$, and $ABB$ faces with nonconstant spectators, and compare the chosen analytic representatives with the original finite-height/circular-hole product. The scalar continuation in (B8) does not do that assembly: changing $g$ changes the full separated permutation problem. A proof cannot substitute the scalar identity for the missing full-word comparison or presume that all finite normalization choices agree.

The next concrete calculation is therefore the embedded proper-face subtraction sum, with (B11)–(B17) and the ultraviolet $AB$ boundary-value extension retained. If it closes, the higher Taylor levels of the primitive full cluster have positive relative degree and one can formulate a complete first-Yukawa contour statement with its exact scope. Only then should the mass-squared Serre test be attempted, with the composite potential, double Yukawa insertions, and single-current counterterms combined. Those diagrams are not calculated here.

Separately, the mixed relation still has a fermionic boundary operator whose massive spatial boundary values are not known. This report supplies no physical boundary closure, coproduct, faithful presentation, or Hilbert-space charge construction. Neither affine $\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)$ nor affine $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ has been established.
